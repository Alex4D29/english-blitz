# WarioWare English

A WarioWare-style collection of fast English microgames for Japanese kids learning the alphabet, phonics and reading. Each microgame lasts a few seconds: flash an instruction, the player acts, win or lose, then the next game starts, a little faster.

Audience: young Japanese children, many of whom can't read English yet (and some can't read much Japanese either). Design for kids: big targets, very little reading needed to play, instant and obvious feedback.

## Current state

The shared frame is built and sixty-five microgames plug into it (see also **Tutorial** below):

- **Balloon Pop** (`games/balloon-pop.js`, "POP THE LETTER!" / "POP THE WORD!"): the voice says "B for ball" and letter balloons drift around, bouncing off each other. The player pops the right one. A right pop gives confetti and the rest float away. A wrong pop bursts into green slime, then the right balloon wiggles. Faster speeds make the balloons drift faster, with 6 balloons at 2× and up. Words longer than 4 letters shrink to fit the balloon. Phonics list ("POP THE PICTURE!"): the button at the top shows the letter (B 🔊) and the voice says just the letter. One balloon carries a picture that starts with it, and the others carry pictures from other letters (look-alike letters first). After the pop, or the reveal, the voice says "B for ball". Takes letter, phonics, cvc and hfw lists.
- **Wire Cut** (`games/wire-cut.js`, "CUT THE WIRE!", a rhythm game; rebuilt from Robot Boss's finale, which the teacher liked better): a silly bomb (black ball, grey cap, a fizzing fuse, googly eyes, a worried little mouth, throbbing) on the right of a dark lab grid, with its countdown screen on its belly showing the target (revealed big first; tap it to hear it again). A column of plugs on the left holds the options from `ctx.choiceSteps(3, N, { lowercase: true })` (3, or 4 from 1.75×), each with a colour-coded wire to the bomb, crossed (follow the colours). Tap a plug or its wire to cut it (a gap opens, ✂️). Right: SNIP!, a ✂️ pip, then the beat: the clock stops, the screen flips to a new target, the voice says it, fresh wires plug in. Wrong: ⚡, the plug turns red, the bomb ticks faster and swells (TICK TICK!), a ✕ pip; that wire stays cut, so with 3 wires the right one is all that's left after 2 misses. 3 cuts: defused (the fuse goes out with a 💨, PHEW!, the screen turns green, the bomb hops, confetti). 3 misses or a timeout: BOOM (flash, shake, 💥, everything goes sooty) and the right plug glows. History: it was "cut the one wrong pair", then "cut the one matching pair (A—a, 🐱—cat)"; under time pressure both caused panic and confusion in playtesting, so the unique pair-matching mechanic was dropped. Keys 1–4 cut, Space replays. Time limit is 12 s (the beats don't count); 14 s as Robot Boss's finale (`bossDuration`), where the same code draws the robot instead (see Boss Battle). Takes letter, phonics, cvc and hfw lists.
- **Feed Me** (`games/feed-me.js`, "FEED ME!"): a hungry picture (🍎) wants its letter or word, and the player drags the right tile into its mouth. The voice says only the word ("apple"), so the player has to find the first letter. After a miss, it says "A for apple". With the phonics list it skips items whose word doesn't start with the letter (X/fox). Faster speeds add a 4th tile, then the creature sways. It needs `emoji`, so it only takes phonics and cvc lists.
- **Train Builder** (`games/train-builder.js`, "BUILD THE TRAIN!" / "SPELL THE TRAIN!"): a steam engine shows a letter (M) and the player taps carriages to hook on the next three in order (N, O, P). The engine starts anywhere from A to W. With word lists, the engine shows the picture (or, for sight words, the word itself), the voice says "cat", and the player spells it c-a-t (words up to 5 letters). A wrong carriage shakes and play goes on (no penalty). On timeout the right ones roll into place. When the train is complete it toots and steams off. Faster speeds add distractor carriages: the letter just after the run, then look-alikes. Time limit is 7 s. Phonics list ("BUILD THE TRAIN!"): the engine shows a letter (C), and the tray has 3 pictures that start with it plus 1 wrong picture (2 from 1.5×). Right pictures can go on in any order: a tapped right picture is swapped into the next slot's place in `needed`, so the timeout reveal still fills the rest. Letters with fewer than 3 such pictures (X) are never the engine. Takes letter, phonics, cvc and hfw lists.
- **Trace Monster** (`games/trace-monster.js`, "TRACE THE MONSTER!"): a fat monster shaped like a letter, capital or lowercase (googly eyes, toothy grin) appears and the voice says the letter. The player drags over it, and dots along each stroke light up. A dot lights when the finger passes within 11 units of it (the letter box is 100). When every stroke is 90% lit, the monster swells and pops into goo, and its eyeballs bounce away. Stroke order isn't shown or enforced, because schools teach it differently. A ghost 👆 slides along one stroke to show what to do. A list item written in lowercase is always lowercase; otherwise the case is a coin flip. During the round the monster gets fatter and its mouth wider. On timeout it lunges and CHOMPs the screen. Letter shapes are polylines in `LETTERS` (A–Z and a–z, lowercase on handwriting lines). Faster speeds tighten the tolerance, and at 1.75× and up the dotted guide is hidden. **Three monsters to win, one after another** (the teacher found one too quick): each pop lights a 👾 pip (top left), the clock stops for a beat (`pauseTimer`), and the next monster hops in; hunger grows over the whole round (`timeFrac`), so later ones start fatter; the ghost 👆 only shows on the first. Time limit is 18 s. Takes letter lists only.
- **Whack-a-Mole** (`games/whack-a-mole.js`, "WHACK THE LETTER!" / "WHACK THE WORD!"): the target sits in a card at the top (tap it to hear it again), and the voice says it. Moles pop out of holes for about 2 s, each holding its letter or word on its belly. Some have the target and some don't. Whack 3 targets to win (⭐ pips, top left). Whack 3 wrong moles and they laugh, the 💣 swells (pips top right), and on the third the bomb explodes: flash, blast, shake, and every mole pops up covered in soot. Tapping an empty hole just whiffs the 🔨. Letter lists use a 3×3 field, word lists a 2×3 field with wider moles, and the sign text is measured so the longest word fits. A target is forced after 2 non-targets in a row, and also whenever no target is showing and time is short (less than 1.5 s / pace left per remaining hit). Faster speeds shorten how long moles stay up, spawn them more often, and allow 3 up at once from 1.5×. Time limit is 11 s (it needs 3 hits). Phonics list ("WHACK THE PICTURES!"): the card shows the letter (B), and the moles hold pictures on a 3×3 field. Any picture that starts with B counts. All of the letter's pictures take turns (⚽ 🐝 🍌 🐻), and the 6 wrong ones come from other letters. Every whack, right or wrong, says the picture's word. Sooty moles hide their emoji (emoji ignore `color:transparent`). Takes letter, phonics, cvc and hfw lists.
- **Find My Baby** (`games/find-my-baby.js`, "FIND MY BABY!"): a big capital-letter monster (M) with eyes on stalks sobs for its baby, with a 👶❓ bubble, and the voice says "Where is my baby? M!". Lowercase babies (m, n, w, v) wander around the bottom, crying, with tears and quiet synth sobs. The player drags the right baby up to the monster: it hops in beside the big letter, both stop crying, and hearts and confetti burst out. A wrong baby makes the monster shake its head ("No!"), the baby is tossed back, and the real one glows. Dropping a baby anywhere else is free. Tapping a baby makes it say its letter name, and tapping the monster repeats the prompt. Faster speeds add babies (5 at 1.5×, 6 at 2×) and make them wander faster. Time limit is 7 s. Takes letter lists only.
- **Shuriken Toss** (`games/shuriken-toss.js`, "HIT THE LETTER!" / "HIT THE WORD!"; the ninja is drawn in CSS: a black hood, a big skin-coloured eye slit with big mismatched googly eyes, a red headband with flapping tails, and X eyes when knocked out): the target sits in a card at the top (tap it to hear it again), and the voice says it. A cannon on the left fires letter balls in slow arcs across a dusk sky (about 4 s to cross at 1×). Tapping a ball makes the ninja (bottom right) throw a homing shuriken at it, so the player never has to lead a moving target. Taps get 22 px of slop, and balls already aimed at are skipped. Tapping empty sky is a free miss, and balls that fly off cost nothing. A target ball is sliced in half diagonally and the halves fall. Slice 3 to win (⭐ pips). A wrong ball clinks the shuriken off, turns red and drops (💥 pips). On the third wrong hit the ninja is knocked out (😵) and everything falls. The first shot is always a target, then 50% odds, and a target is forced after 2 non-targets in a row (1 from 1.75×). Faster speeds fly and fire faster, with 4 balls in the air from 1.5×. Time limit is 13 s. Phonics list ("HIT THE PICTURES!"): like Whack-a-Mole's phonics mode. The card shows a letter, the balls carry pictures, and you hit 3 that start with it. Each hit says the word. Takes letter, phonics, cvc and hfw lists.
- **Claw Catch** (`games/claw-catch.js`, "CATCH IT!"): a hungry letter monster (big colored B, googly eyes, open mouth) above a top-down claw machine says "I want B!" (tap it to hear it again). The floor is a grid of picture prizes, with exactly one right answer: a picture whose word starts with the letter (X/fox-style items are never the target, and no distractor starts with the letter). The claw starts in a dashed lane at the left side of the floor, so it never covers a picture while it slides up and down along a yellow gantry bar (the bar runs under the prizes). Meanwhile the whole row it's level with glows softly. The player presses the big red STOP button (or taps the machine, or Space/Enter). Then the claw leaves the lane and sweeps left and right over the prizes (never back into the lane), and the player presses STOP again. The prize under it glows. A second STOP within 250 ms is ignored, so a double tap can't skip the second step. The result is reported the moment of the second STOP, and the claw grabs the nearest prize in its cell. Then the claw snaps to the cell centre, opens, drops (shrinks, shadow merges), closes and lifts. A right prize is carried up and flies into the monster's mouth: chomp, hearts, "B for ball" (it chomps at about 0.94 s, before the frame clears at 1.4 s, so keep that sequence short). The monster is shown big first (`ctx.reveal(mon)`; the teacher asked for the target to be clear at the start), and the claw waits until that's over (`holdUntil`). A wrong prize slips back with a bounce, the monster shakes its head and the right prize glows. Word lists: the monster holds the word ("cat") and wants its picture. Faster speeds move the claw faster, with 4 prizes (2×2) at 1× and 6 (3×2) from 1.5×. The claw steps by frame time, capped at 50 ms per frame. Time limit is 9 s. Needs `emoji`, so phonics and cvc lists only.
- **Gone Fishing** (`games/fishing.js`, "CATCH IT!"): a monster with a fishing rod stands on a dock above a pond. Phonics list: the monster is the letter (A), says "I want A!" and wants the fish carrying a picture that starts with it (🍎; X/fox-style items are never the target, and no other fish starts with the letter). CVC lists: the monster holds the word ("cat") and wants the 🐱 fish. HFW lists: the monster is made of the word (shrunk for long words; it used to be a plain blob that only said it, so with no voice there was nothing to go on) with a 🔊 bubble, and the fish carry words, with look-alikes first (into: it, to, here). Tap the monster to hear it again. The monster (and what it wants) is shown big first (`ctx.reveal(mon)`; the teacher asked for the target before the round begins). The player swipes into the pond (or just taps it). A dotted arc and ring show the aim, and the bobber lands where the finger lets go, clamped inside the pond. There's no ghost 👆 for the cast (playtesting: not needed). The nearest fish within about 45% of the pond width comes over to the bait, one at a time. While it bites (the bobber dips, ❗ and a small 👆TAP! pill pop up, plip sounds) the player taps it (28 px slop, the ❗ 👆TAP! sign above it, or within 60 px of the bobber) to reel it up to the monster. Playtesting showed kids didn't know to tap again after the bite without the TAP! pill. A biting fish is raised above the others so the sign isn't covered. A right fish wins the moment it's tapped. Then the monster jumps, with hearts and confetti, and says "A for apple". A wrong fish makes the monster cry (😢, tears, head shake). It's tossed back after 0.7 s, then the prompt repeats and the player can cast again (no penalty other than time). An untapped biter swims off after 1.5 s and the next nearby fish comes (one that just bit waits 2.5 s). Casting again sends the current fish away. Taps on a fish that's on its way to the bait are ignored, so they don't scare it off. On timeout the monster cries and the right fish glows. Fish carry pictures for phonics and cvc lists (sized to about a third of the pond width), and words for HFW (shrunk to fit). Faster speeds make fish swim and come faster, shorten the bite (1.5 s / pace, where pace is 0.6 + 0.4 × speed), and add fish: 4 at 1×, 5 from 1.5×, 6 from 2×. Time limit is 12 s. Takes phonics, cvc and hfw lists.
- **Egg Shuffle** (`games/egg-shuffle.js`, "FOLLOW THE EGG!"): the card at the top shows the target from the start and the voice says it ("B for ball" / "cat"). Letters or words stand in a row as little characters (colored body, googly eyes, legs). Giant speckled eggs drop over them with a thud, and the letters are hidden once the eggs land. Then the eggs shuffle with swaps: one egg hops over, the other ducks under. The first swap always moves the target's egg, and the same pair never swaps twice in a row. The player only has to follow the target's egg. After the last swap the card slams again, the voice repeats the target, and the eggs start wobbling. Only now can they be tapped (keys 1–4 also work, left to right). Tap the card to hear it again. A right egg cracks along a zigzag, the top half flies off, and the letter hops out onto the bottom shell, with cheeps and confetti. Then the other eggs crack, dimmed, so the player sees what was where. A wrong egg is rotten: green shell, stink waves, 💨 and a rude noise. Then the right egg cracks open and glows. On timeout every egg cracks and the right one glows. All the eggs look the same, speckles included. The watching part (stand, drop, swaps) is fitted into about 60% of the normal time limit (`ctx.paceMs`), and choosing gets the rest of the real limit (never less than 1.8 s), so warm-up rounds just give longer to choose. Swaps are capped at 0.8 s each. Faster speeds squeeze the watching part, and from 2× there are 4 eggs and 4 swaps (3 of each before that). Words are capped at 5 letters (4 when there are 4 eggs). Time limit is 10 s. Takes letter, cvc and hfw lists.
- **Clean the Room** (`games/clean-room.js`, "CLEAN THE ROOM!"): a messy bedroom (striped wallpaper, window, wood floor, pink rug). Lowercase letters lie on the rug at small tilts (never more than 18°, so b/d/p/q stay themselves). A cardboard box with a capital letter on it (A) stands against the wall, and googly eyes peer out of its opening and follow a dragged letter. The voice says the box letter ("A", or "B and D"). The player drags every lowercase a into box A. 3 copies are on the floor, plus 3 decoys (`tricky` look-alikes first). A right letter arcs into the box: gulp, a green pip on the box, and the voice says its name. A wrong letter makes the box shake, say "BLEH!" and spit it back onto the rug with a spin. That only costs time. A letter dropped on the wall slides back down onto the floor, and anywhere else it just stays put. Tap a letter to hear its name, and tap a box to hear its letter. When every box is full, the boxes close, confetti flies, and a 🧹 swishes across the floor and sends the leftover letters flying off the right edge, with ✨. On timeout the uncollected letters glow and the boxes shake. From 1.5× there are two boxes, usually a look-alike pair from `tricky` (B and D, M and N), each needing 2 letters, plus 2 decoys (3 from 2×). Box ink is blue or pink, and the letters never use those colors so color can't hint at a box. A lowercase l is never put beside box I. Keyboard: Enter or 1 puts the focused letter in the first box, 2 in the second. Time limit is 10 s. Takes letter lists only.
- **Samurai Slice** (`games/samurai-slice.js`, "SLICE THE MATCH!" / "SLICE THE PICTURE!" / "SLICE THE WORD!", input `slice`): a samurai (red headband with flapping tails, angry brows, katana) stands in the middle of a dashed ring. Their white chest plate shows the prompt, and they say it. Bandits (white discs with colored rims and angry eyes) rush in from off screen, then surround the samurai on an ellipse and creep slowly closer. The player swipes across the right bandit. A move only counts as a slice after 22 px of travel. The first long-enough move is checked from where the finger went down, so a quick flick through a bandit counts, and the bandit nearest the swipe line within its radius is hit. A white trail follows the finger. Right slice: the samurai spins the sword, a white slash flashes along the swipe, the bandit splits into two halves cut along the swipe angle (cloned into two clip-path halves inside a rotated wrapper), and confetti bursts. Wrong slice: a clang, that bandit goes grey and sad (brows flip, 💧 tear, droops), the samurai shakes their head with 💦, and the right bandit glows. Slice 3 to win (⭐ pips, top left; the teacher found one slice over too quickly): the target stays the same, and each right slice sends the rest of the wave running and brings a fresh wave rushing in (new distractors; phonics: a different picture of the letter, from `ctx.phonics.set(letter, 3, …)`), and the samurai says the target again. A wrong slice still loses. A tap doesn't slice: on the samurai it replays the prompt, and anywhere else it shows the SLICE! hint again at that spot (at most every 1.2 s). ABC: the samurai is a capital or lowercase letter (coin flip) and the bandits show the other case, with look-alikes from `pickOptions`. I and L are never on the same board, because I and l look the same. PHONICS: the samurai is the letter, the bandits hold pictures from `ctx.phonics.set(letter, 1, n−1)`, and the answer is spoken "B for ball". CVC: the samurai holds the word (shrunk to fit), and the bandits hold pictures, with look-alike words first (tap: ⚡ zap, 🗺️ map, 😴 nap). HFW: the samurai shows 🔊 and only says the word; the bandits are wide pills (1.8 × 0.86 of the bandit size) so 7-letter words like "because" fit. The word size is guessed from its length, then shrunk after layout if it still overflows, and hits are tested against each bandit's own half-width and half-height, so pills slice like circles do. The right answer is always put on the board before distractors are trimmed. The in-game SLICE! hint is small and finger-only (`{ small: true }`), on the samurai's bottom edge below the plate. Spots out on the ring always ended up next to some bandit on a crowded board, which reads as "slice this one". Faster speeds: 4 bandits at 1×, 5 at 1.5×, 6 at 2×, and from 1.5× they slowly circle the samurai. Time limit is 12 s (it was 7 s for one slice). Takes letter, phonics, cvc and hfw lists.
- **Ghost Hunt** (`games/ghost-hunt.js`, "FIND THE GHOST!", input `drag`): a dark room (black outside the beam, so nothing on the ghosts can be read). A card at the top shows the target (letter; phonics letter; CVC word; HFW 🔊) and the voice says it. Ghosts drift about, hidden in the dark at 1× and 1.25×–1.5×: their eyes only show in the beam (`.seen`) or once the lights are on (glowing eyes made it too easy at 1×); from 1.75× (Practice MEDIUM and HARD) the eyes glow in the dark again (`.gh-room.eyes`), since hidden ghosts were too tricky that fast, and they're a little smaller than at first (24% × 19% of the room at 3 ghosts). The player drags a flashlight beam that follows the finger with a little lag (`1 - exp(-5·dt)`; 7 was a bit quick for the teacher) (it sits half a beam above the fingertip on touch, so the finger doesn't cover it; a mouse moves it by hovering). Ghosts carry letters, pictures (phonics: one starts with the letter; CVC: the word's picture) or words (HFW). A wrong ghost lit for 0.12 s gets scared (shakes, squeals) and flees off screen, which costs nothing. On the right ghost a yellow ring fills over 1.3 s of beam, then drains 1.5× as fast once the beam leaves; when it's full the ghost POPS, the lights come on and the others flee. Nothing reacts to the beam until the player first moves it, so a ghost drifting into the starting beam can't win or flee by itself. On timeout the lights come on and the right ghost glows. Hint: a small finger-only drag from the beam. Faster speeds: 3 ghosts, 4 from 1.5×, 5 from 2×, drifting faster. Time limit is 10 s. Takes letter, phonics, cvc and hfw lists.

- **Window Wiper** (`games/window-wiper.js`, "WIPE THE WINDOW!", input `drag`, icon 🪟): the cleaner, a bucket-of-water monster holding a yellow cloth (inline SVG, the cloth scrubs), stands beside a card with the target (letter; phonics letter; CVC word; HFW 🔊), and the voice says it. ## Architecture

A single-page web app in plain HTML/CSS/JS, with no build step and no framework. Everything is a classic `<script>`, because ES modules don't load from `file://`. It must keep working when you open `index.html` directly or serve it from any static host.

```
index.html          screens markup + script tags (no inline logic)
css/frame.css       frame styles
js/text.js          TEXT: all frame UI strings
js/core.js          registerMicrogame(), audio unlock + SFX, speech, storage, helpers (window.Core)
js/music.js         chiptune background music (window.Music)
js/power.js         the power bar mechanic (window.PowerBar, ctx.powerBar)
js/modes.js         MODES (fun, challenge, boss, practice) and SCORING
js/mascot.js        the mascot, "you": a chunky orange monster (window.Mascot: el(size), mood(el, m))
js/boss.js          Boss Battle: the bosses (CSS characters), their stages and cut-scenes (window.Boss)
js/frame.js         screens, round loop, timer, HUD, results, game loading
data/lists.js       word lists (window.WORD_LISTS)
games/manifest.js   GAME_FILES: the list of microgames to load
games/*.js          one file per microgame
```

Test URLs: `index.html?game=wire-cut` plays only that game (the content picker still shows). `?list=cvc` plays only that list and skips the content picker (lists: `letters-az`, `phonics-az`, `cvc`, `hfw`). They can be combined.

### Modes (`js/modes.js`)

- **FUN MODE:** 10 games, no lives, speed stays at 1×. A miss only lowers accuracy. Results show games cleared out of 10, accuracy, and 1–3 stars (≥90% gets 3 stars, ≥60% gets 2, otherwise 1).
- **BOSS BATTLE:** 10 levels like Fun, then a boss: a best-of-3 showdown at 1.25× and a 3-stage final battle (1.5×, 1.75×, 2×). See **Boss Battle** above.
- **ENDLESS MODE** (was CHALLENGE MODE; the code still says challenge): 4 lives, and a lost game costs one. It opens with a warm-up (`warmup` in `js/modes.js`), so young players can learn the games without pressure: rounds 1–5 get at least 20 s and rounds 6–10 at least 15 s, all at 1×. From round 11 it plays like before: 1× with each game's own time limit, then speed goes up by 0.25 every 5 games (first SPEED UP! at round 16), up to 2.5×. The run lasts until the lives run out. Results show games cleared, accuracy and a speed score. Each cleared game scores 10–100 points: `100 × (1 − timeMs / timeLimit)`, with a minimum of 10. The best speed score is saved in `localStorage` under `wwe.challenge.best`.
- **PRACTICE:** one chosen game, over and over, with no lives and no end, at the difficulty picked on the How to play card: 🐢 EASY (1×), 🐇 MEDIUM (1.75×) or 🚀 HARD (2.5×, Challenge's top speed), saved in `localStorage` under `wwe.practice.speed` and shown in the HUD where the round number usually is (tutorial demos still run at 1×). The "ROUND n" banner is skipped. The HUD shows `✅ cleared / played` and a ❓ button that goes back to the How to play card. ✕ goes back to the game picker. There's no results screen.
- **No repeats** (the teacher: there are enough games now): `state.played` holds the games played this run, and `chooseRound` picks an unplayed one (a content type that still has one, if the picked type has run out); the showdown prefers unplayed games too; battle stages are fixed. Endless mode starts over once every game has been played; elsewhere, if the content in play runs out of new games, a repeat is allowed (never the last game).
- **Endless points banner:** between games Endless says GET READY! (first), ONE POINT! / TWO POINTS! … (spelled out to twenty, then digits) after a win, or TRY AGAIN! after a miss, instead of ROUND n (rounds counted the misses too, which confused the teacher); the HUD pill shows ⭐ points.
- Fun and Challenge pick a ticked content type at random each round, then a game that can use it, never the same game twice in a row.

### The shared frame (`js/frame.js`)

**Only the frame** handles:

- **The transition:** "SPEED UP!" when the speed changes, then "ROUND n", then the game's instruction slams in with a background flash (1.3 s). Above it, the game's `icon` pops in big (👮, 👻, 🎈…), so children who can't read can still tell which game is coming. There used to be a 👆 TAP! / DRAG! / SLICE! pill under it, but playtesting showed 5-year-olds took it as "tap now!", tapped during the banner and hit a random answer the moment the game appeared, so the banner no longer shows the gesture (the in-game hint still does). Every game must still declare `input`, because the in-game hint uses it. **Start lock:** for the first 0.5 s of every game (`START_LOCK_MS`) `#game` gets `.locked` (no pointer events), so a tap carried over from the banner can't pick an answer (longer when the game reveals its target, see below). The time limit starts counting after the lock. A new color theme each round.
- **Target reveal (`ctx.reveal(card)`):** games with one target shown in a small card zoom that card up big in the middle of the game for a moment before play (`REVEAL_MS` = 1.4 s: pops in, holds, flies back into place), with everything else dimmed by a huge spread `box-shadow` and a two-note chime. The teacher asked for it because in games like Shuriken Toss kids couldn't react without first taking in the target. It animates the card element itself (on top of its own transform), so the game's styles stay. Touches stay locked and the clock waits until it's over (the start lock becomes `REVEAL_MS + 60`), and the game holds back anything that moves on its own by the returned ms (Shuriken's first shot, Whack-a-Mole's first mole, Mouse Trap's first mouse, Sack Man's scissors). It ends on a timer, not the animation's `finish` event, which stalls in throttled tabs. Used by: Shuriken Toss, Whack-a-Mole, Mouse Trap, Ghost Hunt, Window Wiper, Sack Man, Froggy Hop, Monkey Swing, Snowman, Train Tracks, Bunny Burrow, Pizza Chef, and Balloon Pop in phonics (elsewhere its card is only 🔊). Not used where the target is already big and central (Feed Me, Find My Baby, Claw Catch, Gone Fishing, Samurai Slice, Clean the Room, Stop the Sneeze, the Imposter poster) or hidden on purpose (Egg Shuffle, Puzzle Reveal).
- **The in-game hint (`ctx.hint`):** the same 👆 TAP! / DRAG! / SLICE! appears again over the game for 2.4 s, where the game points it, and vanishes at the first touch. A drag hint slides from `from` to `to`. Near the bottom of the screen the word moves above the fingertip. **Never point it at one answer option**, because a child takes that as "tap this one". Playtesting found the in-game hint obnoxious where the game already explains itself, so it's used sparingly and kept out of the way. **No in-game hint:** Balloon Pop, Whack-a-Mole, Shuriken Toss, Gone Fishing (no first-cast ghost either, but its 👆TAP! pill on a biting fish stays) and Trace Monster (it has its own ghost 👆). **Where the rest point it:** Train Builder: in the gap between the track and the carriages, 0.65×, TAP! beside the finger. Feed Me: a 0.6× finger only, centred just above the tiles, sliding up to the mouth. Clean the Room: 0.65×, from the rug to the box. Find My Baby: finger only. Claw Catch: a 0.75× finger only, pressing the right end of STOP (beside the word). Egg Shuffle: 0.7×, centred a little below the row of eggs, TAP! beside the finger. Samurai Slice: small, on the samurai's bottom edge. Options: `gap: true` aims between the two options nearest the middle; `edge: "bottom"`; `small: true` (half size, finger only); `noWord: true` (finger only); `scale: n`; `side: true` (word to the right of the finger, for a narrow horizontal gap); `center: true` (the point is the finger's middle, not its tip, to sit it inside a gap). The finger is always kept on screen (its tip at most 80 px × scale from the bottom).
- **The timer bar:** green → orange → red, with ticks in the last 2 s. Running out of time counts as a loss.
- **Lives and progress:** hearts in Challenge, 10 progress pips in Fun.
- **Feedback between games:** happy sound or buzz, vibration, and a YES! / NOPE! / TOO SLOW! stamp. After the 1.4 s feedback, the next round waits for any speech still playing ("A for apple") to finish, up to 2.5 s more (`whenQuiet`).
- **Audio unlock:** tapping a mode button or a content toggle creates the `AudioContext`, and PLAY calls `speechSynthesis`. iOS blocks sound until the first tap.
- **Results, high score, PLAY AGAIN and MENU.**
- **Clearing every timer** a game started, through `ctx.later` / `ctx.raf`, between games.
- **No accidental zooming:** the viewport has `maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=no`, `html` has `touch-action: manipulation`, and since iPad Safari ignores `user-scalable=no`, frame.js also cancels `gesturestart/change/end` (pinch), `dblclick`, two-finger `touchmove`, and a second `touchend` within 300 ms (double-tap zoom).
- **A scroll bar you can see** (`updateScrollbar`): iPad Safari hides scroll bars until you scroll, so any menu screen (`.screen.scroll`) that can scroll gets a fixed track and thumb down the right edge, and a bouncing ⬇️ button at the bottom while there's more below (tap it to scroll down 70% of a screen). Both hide when the screen fits.
- **Speech on iPad** (`Core.say`): iOS Safari often drops an utterance spoken right after `speechSynthesis.cancel()`, which made the round-start prompt silently disappear when the last line was still playing (reported in Gone Fishing). So when something is still speaking, the new line waits 90 ms after the cancel; a newer `say()` wins.

Robustness:
- If a game throws in `start()`, it's logged and skipped for the session, and the round is replayed with no penalty.
- Leaving the tab mid-round cancels that round, and it's replayed when the player comes back.

A microgame never draws its own timer, lives, score or result screens.

### Microgame contract

```js
registerMicrogame({
  id: "my-game",
  name: { en: "Tap It" },                    // shown in the Practice picker
  icon: "👆",                                // one emoji for the Practice picker and How to play card
  howTo: { en: {                             // Practice mode's How to play card; or (list) => ({ en: … })
    steps: [["👂", "Listen to the word."], ["👆", "Tap it!"]],   // [emoji, short sentence], 2–4 of them
    tips: ["Tap 🔊 to hear it again."],      // optional: hints, what a mistake does, what speeding up changes
  } },
  instruction: { en: "TAP IT!" },            // or (list) => ({ en: … }) to vary by list type
  contentTypes: ["letter", "cvc"],           // which list types it can use
  duration: 5000,                            // base time limit in ms at speed 1 (default 5000)
  input: "tap",                              // "tap", "drag" or "slice": the word ctx.hint shows (TAP! / DRAG! / SLICE!; add new gestures to TEXT.input + frame.css)
  tutorial: true,                            // required: it calls ctx.tutorial(…) in start (see Tutorial)
  listening: true,                           // only if the target is never shown, only spoken: left out when listening games are off
  // Boss Battle (optional): bossRival (draws the boss into the showdown: Boss.rival), bossArt (draws the boss
  // itself in the final battle: Boss.fighting), bossDuration / bossContentTypes (the boss version's own time
  // limit / list types).
  start(ctx) {
    // …build the game in ctx.root…
    return () => { /* optional cleanup: remove document listeners, observers */ };
  },
});
```

`ctx` contains:

| field | meaning |
|---|---|
| `root` | element to render into. The frame empties it afterwards. |
| `items` | the current list's items, freshly shuffled. An item with `pics` arrives with one picked at random already copied into `word` / `emoji`; the full `pics` array is still there. |
| `list` | `{ id, type }` of the current list |
| `speed` | 1, 1.25 … 2.5. Scale difficulty with it. |
| `timeLimit` | ms for this round (`duration / speed`, or longer in the Challenge warm-up) |
| `hint(from, to?, opts?)` | show 👆 TAP! / DRAG! over the game for a moment. `from`/`to`: an element, an array of elements, or `{x, y}` px in `root`. `opts`: `gap: true` (aim between options), `edge: "bottom"`, `small`, `noWord`, `scale: n`, `side`, `center` (see the in-game hint above). Call it once layout exists (e.g. `ctx.later(() => ctx.hint(…), 80)`). |
| `paceMs` | the normal limit (`duration / speed`). Pace animations on this, not `timeLimit`, so a 20 s warm-up round doesn't play in slow motion (Egg Shuffle does this). |
| `demo` | true while the game plays itself in the tutorial (see Tutorial above). Usually only needed to skip something that would get in the way. |
| `tutorial(answer, run)` | **required** (with `tutorial: true`): call it once at the end of `start` (it does nothing outside a demo). `answer`: the list item (or array of items) this round is about, so the child's round gets a different one (`choiceRows` returns it as `target`). `run(f)`: an async script for the ghost finger. A target `t` is an element, `{ x, y }` px in `root`, or a function returning either (read every frame, so the finger follows moving things). Moves: `f.point(t, ms?)` glides there and rings it; `f.tap(t)`; `f.drag(from, to, ms?, hold?)` (`hold`: stay pressed on `to`, following it, e.g. Ghost Hunt's beam); `f.slice(from, to)` (a quick swipe); `f.path([t…], msPerSegment)` (press and drag through points: tracing, scrubbing); `f.follow(t, test, max)` (ride along on a moving thing, ringed, until `test()`: Egg Shuffle); `f.move(t)` (glide, no touch); `f.tapNow(t)` (press at once, for timing: Claw Catch's STOP, Mouse Trap's button). When a move names an element, the press always goes to that element, even if something else is under the fingertip for a moment (Monster Boxing's pads flip as they change); `f.until(test, max)` (wait for the game, resolves true/false); `f.wait(ms)`. Point at the prompt first, then do the right move, and wait for the game between steps (`until(() => !busy …)`). If the outcome animates after the move (Pachinko's ball), `until` it's done: the frame calls a demo missed 3.5 s after the script ends. |
| `reveal(card)` | for games with one target in a card: call once at the end of `start` with that card element. It's shown big in the middle for 1.4 s first (see Target reveal). Returns the ms, so the game can delay anything that starts moving by itself: `ctx.later(fire, 500 + ctx.reveal(card))`. |
| `win()` / `lose()` | report the result. Only the first call counts. The frame measures the time taken. |
| `ended` | true once a result is in, including a timeout. Check it in input handlers. |
| `onTimeout(fn)` | optional: called when time runs out, so the game can reveal the answer (several can be registered) |
| `onEnd(fn)` | optional: `fn(won, timedOut)` once the result is in, after the onTimeout handlers (Boss.rival uses it) |
| `timeFrac()` | how far through the time limit, 0..1 (0 during the start lock and reveal; it stops at `freezeTimer`) |
| `pauseTimer(ms)` | stop the clock for a beat, then let it carry on (rhythm games, between targets; the timer bar holds still). Ignored in a demo and after a freeze. |
| `nightTheme()` | turn the whole screen's colour theme dark navy for this round only (Rock-a-Bye); the frame puts the normal theme back when the round ends (`endGame`). |
| `freezeTimer()` | optional: stop the clock once the player's choice is made and only the outcome is still animating (Pachinko's falling ball). The game must still call `win()` / `lose()`; the speed score uses the time at the freeze. |
| `say(text)`, `canSpeak` | shared voice. Without speech, show an emoji/text cue instead. |
| `later(fn, ms)`, `raf(fn)` | timers the frame clears between games. Use these instead of `setTimeout`/`requestAnimationFrame`. |
| `sfx` | `tone`, `sweep`, `noise` synth helpers (plus `happy`, `buzz`…). `audio` is the raw `AudioContext` or null. |
| `confetti(layer, x, y, {count})` | confetti burst at (x, y) px inside a positioned layer. Skipped with reduced motion. |
| `shuffle`, `pick`, `pickOptions(target, n)` | helpers. `pickOptions` returns the target + n−1 distractors from the list: `tricky` first, then look-alike words (one letter different: cat/cot; reversed: was/saw, on/no; one inside the other: of/off). At least one distractor is random when n > 2. |
| `phonics.pics(item)`, `phonics.set(letter, nRight, nWrong)` | phonics helpers (`Core.startPics` / `Core.phonicsSet`). `pics` lists a letter item's pictures whose word starts with it (B → ball, bee…; X → none). `set` returns `{ right, wrong }`: up to nRight of those, and nWrong pictures that don't start with the letter, one per other letter, from its `tricky` letters first. Each picture is `{ word, emoji, letter }`, and no emoji repeats. Use these rather than `item.emoji`, which may not start with the letter (X: fox). |
| `lowerLookalikes(target)` | letter items whose lowercase looks like the target's (b → d, p, h; a → o, e, d), shuffled, never I with l (`Core.lowerLookalikes`, map `LOWER_LOOKALIKE` in `js/core.js`). For games that show lowercase letters, where `tricky` (capitals and sounds) is the wrong idea of look-alike. Used by Spot the Imposter and Froggy Hop. |
| `choiceRows(R, N, opts)` | for sequence games: R rows of N options, each with exactly one right one. Returns `{ cardText, listen, prompt, answer, rows }`, with `rows[r]` a shuffled array of `{ text, kind: "letter" | "pic" | "word", right, spoken }`. `listen` is true when the card is 🔊 (HFW with speech). `opts.lowercase`: half of ABC rounds become capital card → lowercase options (with `lowerLookalikes`). Phonics gives a different picture of the letter on each row. Used by Froggy Hop, Monkey Swing, Snowman, Train Tracks, Bunny Burrow, Pizza Chef, Hoop Shot, Monster Boxing, Archery, Monster Golf, Test Your Strength and Maze Run (`Core.choiceRows`). |
| `matchPairs(N)` | for the draw-a-line games: N pairs `{ item, a, b, say }` (`a` left, `b` right, each `{ text, kind, spoken }`): letter capital → lowercase (look-alikes together), phonics letter → picture, cvc word → picture (`Core.matchPairs`). |
| `choiceSteps(R, N, opts)` | for rhythm games: R steps of N options, each step with a DIFFERENT target. An array of `{ cardText, listen, prompt, answer, target, row }` (`row` as in choiceRows). ABC with `lowercase` picks the case once for the whole round (`Core.choiceSteps`). |

Rules:
- Report win or lose once. If the game reports nothing, the timer runs out and counts as a loss. The game's own win/lose animation can keep playing for about 1.4 s afterwards.
- Ignore input after the result is in (`ctx.ended`, or your own `done` flag).
- Don't hard-code content. Everything the player sees or hears comes from `ctx.items`.
- The game must be playable without reading the instruction: use icons, speech and an obvious goal.
- Inject your own CSS once, with a unique class prefix (`bp-`, `wc-`, `fm-`), and check no other game already uses it (`grep -l '.xx-' games/*.js`), keyframe names included. Pachinko and Penalty Kick both once used `pk-`, so after Pachinko had run, Penalty Kick showed a tiny ball, a plain white goal and no characters (the teacher hit it on a laptop); Penalty Kick is now `pen-`, and Monster Boxing (which shared `mb-` with Find My Baby) is now `box-`. (`bx-` is Boss Battle.)

### Adding a microgame

1. Create `games/<name>.js`. Copy the shape of `games/balloon-pop.js`: an IIFE with its own `TEXT` and `CSS`, then `registerMicrogame({...})`. Put `name`, `icon` and `howTo` in `TEXT` too. The How to play steps are written for whoever reads them to the child: short, concrete, one action each, and they must match what the code actually does (what a wrong answer costs, what can be tapped to replay). Set `input: "tap"` or `"drag"`, and call `ctx.hint` where the player should act (never on one answer option).
2. Give it a tutorial: `tutorial: true` and a `ctx.tutorial(answer, run)` script (see the ctx table). Every game has one, and the Tutorials menu lists them all.
3. Add `"<name>"` to `GAME_FILES` in `games/manifest.js`.
4. Test it alone with `index.html?game=<id>`, and with each list it supports (`&list=…`), including its tutorial (Tutorials menu) winning with each list.

You don't need to touch `index.html` or the frame.

### Word lists

Content lives in data files, separate from game code, so one game can run with letter, CVC, HFW and phonics versions. Each list has a `type`, and items share a common core:

```js
{ id: "letters-az", type: "letter",  items: [{ text: "A", word: "apple", say: "Ay", emoji: "🍎", pics: [{ word: "apple", emoji: "🍎" }, { word: "ant", emoji: "🐜" }, …] }, …] }
{ id: "cvc",         type: "cvc",    items: [{ text: "cat", sounds: ["c", "a", "t"], emoji: "🐱" }, …] }
{ id: "hfw",         type: "hfw",     items: [{ text: "the" }, { text: "and" }, …] }
{ id: "phonics-az", type: "phonics", items: /* the same item objects as letters-az */ }
```

- `text`: what appears on screen. `say` (optional): how to spell it for the voice when TTS misreads it (a lone "A" can come out as "uh", so it's spelled "Ay"; on iPad a lone "e" came out sounding like "I", so E is spelled "Ee").
- `emoji` or an image is how pre-readers get the meaning. Prefer it to words wherever you can.
- Pick distractors from the same list. Prefer ones that are tricky for Japanese learners: L/R, B/V, S/TH, F/H, and b/d/p/q letter shapes.
- `pics` (letters): 3–4 pictures per letter (A: apple, ant, arrow, axe). Each round the frame picks one per item and copies it into `word` / `emoji`, so existing games get variety without knowing about `pics`. A game that wants several pictures of one letter (e.g. "find all the A things") can read `item.pics` directly. Rules for adding one: a clear emoji, not used by any other letter, and not easily named with another letter's word (no 🚌 bus with 🚐 van, no 🎁 "present", no 🐂 "cow").
- The lists today: `letters-az` (26, 93 pictures), `phonics-az` (the same 26 letters and pictures, shared by reference, added at the bottom of `data/lists.js`), `cvc` (142 short-vowel words, 65 with a picture: a picture is only given when it's clear and no other word in the list uses it) and `hfw` (158 Dolch/Fry sight words: the first 100, then colours, 46 more common ones and the numbers two, four, five, seven, eight, nine, which the teacher asked for). No word is in both `cvc` and `hfw`: words that look CVC but don't sound it out (was, his, has) are sight words. Words that sound the same (to/two, for/four) can be in the HFW list together: `Core.soundsLike` (the `SOUND_ALIKE` groups in `js/core.js`) keeps one from ever being a wrong answer to the other (`pickOptions`, and Spot the Imposter's twins), since the listening games would then have two right answers. Add any new sound-alike word to `SOUND_ALIKE`.
- **ABC vs PHONICS.** Both use the same letters, but they teach different things, so they're separate types. `letter` (ABC) games are about letter shapes and names: Balloon Pop, Wire Cut, Train Builder, Trace Monster, Whack-a-Mole, Find My Baby, Shuriken Toss, Egg Shuffle, Clean the Room, Samurai Slice, Ghost Hunt, Puzzle Reveal, Stop the Sneeze, Spot the Imposter, Window Wiper, Sack Man, Pachinko, Mouse Trap, Froggy Hop, Monkey Swing, Snowman, Train Tracks, Bunny Burrow, Pizza Chef, Treasure Dig, Hoop Shot, Penalty Kick, Monster Boxing, Archery, Monster Golf, Test Your Strength and Maze Run. `phonics` games link a letter to the first sound of a picture's word: Feed Me, Claw Catch, Gone Fishing, and the phonics modes of Balloon Pop, Wire Cut, Train Builder, Whack-a-Mole, Shuriken Toss, Samurai Slice, Ghost Hunt, Puzzle Reveal, Stop the Sneeze, Spot the Imposter, Window Wiper, Sack Man, Pachinko, Mouse Trap, Froggy Hop, Monkey Swing, Snowman, Train Tracks, Bunny Burrow, Pizza Chef, Treasure Dig, Hoop Shot, Penalty Kick, Monster Boxing, Archery, Monster Golf, Test Your Strength and Maze Run. In a phonics mode the screen shows the letter and the player picks pictures, with `ctx.phonics` supplying the right and wrong pictures. A new game that asks "which picture starts with B?" takes `phonics`, and one that only needs the letter itself takes `letter`. TTS still can't make letter sounds, so phonics games say letter names and words ("B… ball"). A future list of recorded sounds (digraphs: sh, ch with `audio` clips) would also be type `phonics`.
- Games that need pictures (Feed Me, Claw Catch) use only the items that have `emoji`.

## Speech and audio

- Use the Web Speech API (`speechSynthesis`) for letter names, words and sentences. Pick an `en-US`/`en-GB` voice, and prefer local voices.
- `Core.say` lowercases lone capital letters B–Z before speaking, because some voices (iOS) read "E" as "capital E". "A" is left alone, since a lone "a" reads as "uh".
- **TTS can't make phonics sounds** (/b/, /sh/). Phonics content needs recorded clips (`audio` field). Don't try to fake phonemes with TTS.
- Sound effects are synthesized with Web Audio (oscillators). No audio files are needed for SFX.
- If speech isn't available, show a text or emoji cue so the game is still playable.

## Look and feel

- Bright, bold, loud: saturated colors, thick black outlines, hard drop shadows, chunky heavy type, a spinning sunburst background, a new color theme each round.
- Snappy motion: slam, pop, bounce and shake. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Mobile first: works one-handed in portrait at about 360 px wide. Tap targets are big (around 1/4 of the screen for answer buttons). Handle input on `pointerdown` so taps feel instant. Use `100dvh` and safe-area insets. No pinch-zoom or double-tap delays.
- Light haptics (`navigator.vibrate`) on result when available.

## Conventions

- **Localization:** all UI text goes in a `TEXT` object or strings file, never inline in markup or logic. English content is what's being taught. UI and instruction text may get a Japanese (`ja`) version later, so keep them keyed.
- **Errors:** log with `console.error("context:", err)`. Don't log expected speech interruptions (`interrupted`/`canceled`). Never let an error stop the game loop: fall back and keep going.
- **Timers:** keep track of every `setTimeout`/`requestAnimationFrame` a game or round starts, so the frame can clear them between games. Stale timers from a finished game are the most likely source of bugs.
- Keep each microgame small and independent. If two games need the same helper, move it into `js/core.js` and expose it on `ctx`, not into another game.

## Testing

There's no test setup yet. Check changes by opening `index.html` in a desktop browser (use `?game=` to focus on one game) and on a real phone (iOS Safari and Android Chrome). Speech, audio unlock and touch behavior differ there in ways desktop won't show.

Speech only works after a real tap. If a script clicks the start button, you'll see `speech: not-allowed` errors, and that's expected.

Browser automation tabs are often throttled to about 2 animation frames a second when the window is covered, so games that step by frame time (Claw Catch) barely move. For scripted tests, run `window.requestAnimationFrame = (cb) => setTimeout(() => cb(performance.now()), 16)` in the page before starting.
